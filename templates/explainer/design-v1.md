# Visual direction

## Central metaphor

Choose a visual metaphor that makes this concept easier to understand and differs from the last relevant videos.

## Beat map

Create one entry for every meaningful spoken beat. For each one, identify the narration idea it responds to, its `startRatio` or listened `startSec`, the state before the beat, and the visible state update.

Every beat must include at least one purposeful animation or state change while its narration is active. Make accents respond to the spoken idea through actions such as tracing a route, highlighting the current role or detail, filling a status, updating a label, or changing a decision state. Give important updates a clear visual emphasis, then let them settle so the viewer can read the result. Use layered motion and accent color with restraint. Do not leave a beat as a static panel or add decorative motion unrelated to the narration.

Use the supplied WAV as the clock. Start both narration and visuals together, place beat changes where the spoken idea changes, and adjust `startRatio` values after listening to the recording. Do not claim word-level synchronization without reviewed word timings for that exact WAV. Give the main operation or process the clearest visual priority.

## Motion and layout

Describe the meaningful state changes, connector routes, title hierarchy, subtitle clearance, and safe margins. Review animated paths across their full travel before rendering the final video.
